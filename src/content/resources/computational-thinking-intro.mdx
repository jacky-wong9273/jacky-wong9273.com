## What is Computational Thinking?

Computational thinking is a problem-solving approach that involves breaking down complex problems into manageable parts. It's a foundational concept in the IB Computer Science syllabus.

### The Four Pillars

1. **Decomposition** — Breaking down a complex problem into smaller, more manageable sub-problems.
2. **Pattern Recognition** — Identifying similarities or patterns within and across problems.
3. **Abstraction** — Focusing on the important details while ignoring irrelevant information.
4. **Algorithm Design** — Creating step-by-step solutions to solve each sub-problem.

## Example: Planning a School Event

Let's apply computational thinking to planning a school event:

| Step | Technique | Application |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Decomposition | Break into: venue, catering, invitations, schedule |
| 2 | Pattern Recognition | Similar to previous events — reuse what worked |
| 3 | Abstraction | Focus on core logistics, ignore decoration details for now |
| 4 | Algorithm | Create a timeline with ordered tasks and dependencies |

## Practice Questions

1. Explain how decomposition could help in designing a social media application.
2. Give an example of abstraction in everyday life.
3. Design an algorithm (pseudocode) for making a cup of tea.
